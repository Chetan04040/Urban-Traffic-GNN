Urban Traffic Flow Prediction Using Graph Neural Networks

Capturing Spatio-Temporal Highway Dynamics over Non-Euclidean Road Topologies

🏙️

Smart City Problem

Metropolitan traffic congestion costs hundreds of millions of dollars in wasted fuel and lost productivity. Proactive routing and traffic signal timing require forecasting traffic 15 to 60 minutes into the future.

🕸️

The Graph Representation

Road networks are naturally modeled as graphs $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, W)$ where sensors & intersections are nodes $\mathcal{V}$, and roadways are directed edges $\mathcal{E}$ weighted by road distance.

⚡

Our Core Novelty

We introduce an Attention-Enhanced ST-GCN with Congestion-Focal Loss, specifically resolving the severe class imbalance in traffic datasets where rare congestion bottlenecks are missed by standard models.

Theoretical Justification

Why Use a GNN Instead of CNN or LSTM?

The Geometry of Road Networks: Non-Euclidean vs. Euclidean Modeling

❌ Conventional Approaches

1. 2D Convolutional Networks (CNNs)

Assume regular Euclidean grids (like pixels). In cities, straight-line distance is misleading: two sensors 500m apart might sit on opposite sides of a divided interstate with zero connectivity.

2. Pure Temporal LSTMs / GRUs

Process each sensor's historical time series independently. They are completely spatially blind—when a downstream bridge bottlenecks, an LSTM cannot anticipate upstream shockwaves until the delay hits that sensor.

✅ Proposed Solution: Spatio-Temporal GNN

Dual Spatio-Temporal Mechanism

  • Spatial Dimension: Message-passing graph convolutions $H^{(l+1)} = \sigma(\tilde{D}^{-1/2} \tilde{A} \tilde{D}^{-1/2} H^{(l)} W)$ propagate speed information across connected corridors.
  • Temporal Dimension: Bidirectional GRU + Multi-Head Self-Attention model how traffic evolves across time steps.
  • Physical Grounding: Graph adjacency matrix $W_{ij} = \exp(-\text{dist}(i,j)^2/\sigma^2)$ directly incorporates real physical roadway distances.

Why Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) Fails on Traffic Datasets

🟢 Free Flow: 82.9% 🟡 Moderate: 11.8% 🔴 Severe Jams: 5.3%
The Regression-to-the-Mean Trap: Because 83% of samples are smooth free-flow, standard MSE minimizes overall loss by predicting safe mean speeds (~58 mph), leading to catastrophic under-prediction of traffic jams.

Our Solution: Congestion-Focal Loss

We formulated an asymmetric congestion-weighted loss function:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}} = \frac{1}{N \cdot T} \sum_{i, t} w(y_{it}) \cdot \left( y_{it} - \hat{y}_{it} \right)^2$$ $$w(y) = 1.0 + \alpha \cdot \left[ \frac{\max(0, y_{\text{free}} - y)}{y_{\text{free}}} \right]^\gamma$$
  • Normal free-flow ($y = 65$ mph) receives standard weight $w(y) = 1.0$.
  • Severe congestion ($y = 15$ mph) receives weight $w(y) \approx 4.0\times$ to $5.0\times$.
  • Directly penalizes missing dangerous traffic bottlenecks!

Viva Voce Defense & Q&A

Examiner Q&A & Viva Defense Cheat Sheet

Click any question card to review the theoretical defense points

Q1: Why use a Graph Neural Network instead of an LSTM? ▼
LSTMs are purely temporal models that treat sensors independently or flattened without physical road awareness. Road traffic is non-Euclidean: when a downstream bridge bottlenecks, congestion creates a backward shockwave. Spatio-Temporal GNNs explicitly propagate speed messages across connected road edges, predicting upstream delays before they occur.
Q2: What is the Graph Laplacian and why normalize it? ▼
The unnormalized Laplacian is $L = D - A$. In GCNs, we use the symmetric normalized adjacency $\tilde{D}^{-1/2} \tilde{A} \tilde{D}^{-1/2}$ (with self-loops $\tilde{A} = A + I$). Normalization is essential because road intersections have unequal degrees (some have 2 roads, major interchanges have 6). Without normalization, message signals would explode at hubs and vanish at dead ends.
Q3: How did you resolve the severe class imbalance in traffic speeds? ▼
We designed Congestion-Focal Loss $w(y) = 1.0 + \alpha [(\max(0, y_{\text{free}} - y))/y_{\text{free}}]^\gamma$. When traffic drops below 30 mph, the penalty scales up by $4\times$ to $5\times$. This solved regression-to-the-mean, reducing severe jam error by 29.5% (from 2.626 mph to 1.852 mph).
Q4: How does this scale to an entire city with 10,000+ intersections? ▼
Full-graph matrix multiplication scales quadratically $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$. For mega-cities, we can use Cluster-GCN (partitioning the city into regional road clusters) or GraphSAGE neighbor sampling (computing convolutions on mini-batches of sampled $k$-hop neighbors), reducing computational complexity to linear $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{V}| + |\mathcal{E}|)$.
